# Research: appliance networking after the single-bridge capacity falsification

**Date:** 2026-10-04 | **Researcher:** nw-researcher | **Depth:** comprehensive | **Status:** complete research; replacement feasibility unproved | **Model:** GPT 6.1 Sol, xhigh

## Executive summary

Unikraft's primary extreme-density evidence makes multiple bridges and shared-memory/vsock guest communication substantive alternatives; the [dedicated audit](#unikraftkraftcloud-extreme-density-a-consequential-primary-precedent) distinguishes its real 100000-instance deployment from its disclosed later scaled-to-zero experiment and preserves conflicting current TAP-pool documentation. **Recommend stock-kernel routed TAP as the first bounded native SPIKE under the present Ethernet/TAP contract, keeping the current inherited TAP queue, TCX isolation, host TPROXY/kTLS/splice and singular network owner.** The single Linux bridge premise failed through the real owner; upstream v7.0 has 1023 usable bridge ports. A router removes that switch-port allocator, but stock dynamic neighbor GC defaults to 1024, so a naïve routed replacement can encounter another small ceiling. Exact gateway/MAC/local delivery, owned neighbor admission, independent classifier-loss guarding, cleanup and restart must be pinned and witnessed before acceptance. Existing prefix/map/admission limits and hardware costs remain explicit. [Native factual record](../feature/netns-density-295/deliver/native-e18-bridge-capacity-falsification.md), [bridge source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/bridge/br_if.c), [neighbor source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/core/neighbour.c).

Registered TC/eBPF delivery is the direct alternative if route/neighbor/gateway proof or measured costs reverse the ranking. Upstream Open vSwitch is a genuine, allowed kernel-module switch, but its standard automatic OpenFlow allocator has 32767 ordinary IDs, explicit OpenFlow IDs reach 65279, and Linux's shared kernel datapath has 65534 ordinary vports. Multiple logical OVS bridges share that kernel backer. Bounded multiple Linux bridges remain a comparison option; VALE's inspected 254-port switch is a lower ceiling and fails the single-switch requirement. First-party Cloudflare source supports stock TC/XDP mechanisms and explains observer tradeoffs, while Workers isolates and private Containers/Sandbox host networking disclose no comparable TAP-density proof. [OVS allocator](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/ofproto/ofproto.c), [kernel OVS](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/openvswitch/datapath.c), [VALE](https://github.com/luigirizzo/netmap/blob/389daeaf498e4928e2070e5002590108c84e2f39/sys/dev/netmap/netmap_bdg.h), [Cloudflare published TC program](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c4ba153b6501fa7ccfdc7e57946fb1d6011e36e8/tools/testing/selftests/bpf/progs/test_cls_redirect.c).

The prior spikes were real: two-guest transport/FD/security mechanisms, plus a 16384-allocation **listener/registry** comparison. They never supplied full bridge fanout. The missing gate was allocator inventory and an actual-owner boundary crossing before accepting sole-switch feasibility. The required outcome remains removing old NetSlot/per-VM netns overhead without a lower structural ceiling; 16384 remains a placeholder, not a density promise. No kernel patches/fork, smaller hidden measurement, weakened invariant, implementation or normative replacement edit is proposed. The report records required SPIKE/design decisions; source-backed preference is medium confidence, actual replacement performance/feasibility unproved. ## Why the prior feasibility process missed bridge fanout

### What was tested, and what acceptance added

The original [Part A/B receipt](../feature/netns-density-295/spike/findings.md) genuinely tested **two** Cloud Hypervisor guests, shared-/24 DNS, per-port classification, transparent original destination, mutually authenticated TLS 1.3 and kTLS, absence of direct guest TCP bypass, and cleanup on the native host. It explicitly says the assembly is neither a throughput nor workload-density benchmark. Its failed attempts narrowed a real local-delivery problem: bare `broute` on an addressless TAP failed, while delivery through the bridge's MAC and bridge IP stack worked. This is a required caution for any bridge-free replacement; rewriting a MAC alone is not proven sufficient.

Part C additionally loaded and adopted one shared TCX program on two real TAPs, including source-spoof/map-miss/direct-UDP negative evidence. Part D really did compare a **16384-entry allocation population in userspace registries/listeners** (32768 registry records, two shared listeners versus 32768 per-allocation listeners); its real guest networking journey still used two TAPs. The [Part-C/Part-D receipts](../feature/netns-density-295/spike/findings.md) explicitly scope RSS/timing observations and do not populate 16384 bridge ports. A successful listener population therefore cannot corroborate switch fanout.

The [persistent-FD spike](../feature/netns-density-295/spike/findings-persistent-fd-tap.md), [TAP ioctl/seccomp spike](../feature/netns-density-295/spike/findings-tap-ioctl-seccomp.md) and [MAC/FDB isolation spike](../feature/netns-density-295/spike/findings-mac-fdb-isolation.md) investigate queue/activation and cross-guest delivery mechanisms at bounded populations. The latter reproduced an unprivileged queue holder stealing another guest's frames by changing its TAP's host MAC, then showed an egress destination-MAC gate blocking theft and unknown-unicast leakage. These results remain evidence for those mechanisms; they do not test the bridge's port allocator.

[ADR-0114](../product/architecture/adr-0114-node-local-shared-bridge-guest-network.md) selected one node-local Linux bridge and rejected per-workload bridges using a shared-switch density rationale. [ADR-0117](../product/architecture/adr-0117-initial-shared-bridge-density-target.md) knew Parts A–D were two-guest evidence, reported TCX point measurements and linear attach-time extrapolation, and deferred full-population measurement. Nonetheless its sizing and later E18 profiles assumed the single switch could attach all 16384 TAPs. The inspected [September correctness-recovery prior-art research](networking/netns-density-295-replacement-design-prior-art-comprehensive-research.md) focuses explicitly on D-295-R1–R18, with substantial bridge/FDB and VMM source audit; it does not establish bridge fanout. The older [NIC attachment research](dataplane/guest-nic-attachment-netns-vs-fd-passing-comprehensive-research.md) analyzes FD acquisition, process placement and interception compatibility, and records missing density evidence rather than a bridge population proof.

**Analysis:** the miss is an absent independent **structural feasibility gate between bounded mechanism success and topology acceptance**, not absence of spikes. Before declaring a topology scalable, inventory every identifier allocator on the actual owner effect path, read its bounds, then reproduce a boundary crossing with the exact target build and owner. Port numbers are separate from IPv4 headroom, BPF map capacity, listener count and estimated memory. Those latter quantities could all fit while bridge port allocation refuses. The need for this gate follows directly from the mismatch between the receipts' explicit two-guest scope and ADR-0114/0117's later sole-bridge population premise. This audit does not assign personal blame or claim every historical artifact was exhaustively searched.

## Current production ownership and packet paths

### The packet path that a replacement must preserve

The current BPF ingress classifier looks up the **ingress ifindex**, validates expected guest IPv4 and source MAC, and validates complete Ethernet/IPv4 ARP sender fields. Valid TCP to a guest, gateway or off-subnet Service frontend is classified for interception; its original IP/port remain unchanged. It rewrites Ethernet destination to the node bridge MAC and sets `0x295a`. Valid ARP and permitted non-TCP receive `0x295b`; direct non-TCP to a peer and unregistered/spoofed traffic drop. The corresponding public contract includes `PACKET_HOST` and an independent three-rule bridge-family guard. This description distinguishes the accepted contract from the bounded source primitive, rather than treating source text as full native proof. [Production classifier](../../crates/overdrive-bpf/src/programs/guest_tcx.rs), [ADRs 0115/0139](../product/architecture/adr-0115-tcx-endpoint-classification-feeds-transparent-mtls.md).

The working source-TAP → bridge-local path enters host IPv4 prerouting and leg-F. Leg-F resolves the original destination and allocation capability; leg-B → leg-C uses platform-held SVIDs, mutual TLS 1.3 and kTLS; leg-S originates on the host and reaches the destination guest as intentional plaintext after decryption. Host output rules preserve leg-B diversion and leg-S exemption. Service VIP routing/resolution and shared DNS are existing separate owners; switch replacement is not authority to invent cross-node routing, broker or persistence machinery. [Part A/B](../feature/netns-density-295/spike/findings.md), [ADR-0115](../product/architecture/adr-0115-tcx-endpoint-classification-feeds-transparent-mtls.md), [ADR-0138](../product/architecture/adr-0138-required-serve-boundary-intercept-and-guest-dns-ports.md).

The egress classifier uses the **egressing TAP's ifindex** and registered guest MAC, dropping foreign unicast even when a host-side MAC changed or an FDB floods; broadcast/multicast are allowed by that existing contract. The actual endpoint map has **65536 entries**, not unlimited capacity. [Production map](../../crates/overdrive-bpf/src/maps/guest_tcx.rs), [production egress classifier](../../crates/overdrive-bpf/src/programs/guest_tcx.rs), [ADR-0142](../product/architecture/adr-0142-guest-tap-egress-drops-frames-to-foreign-destination-mac.md). **Confidence: high for checked source/contract facts; replacement equivalence remains unproven.**

The scope is currently IPv4 guest networking. The ingress classifier rejects non-IPv4/non-ARP EtherTypes, so an IPv6 NDP capability must not be inferred from the egress multicast exception. Each candidate below names its IPv6 mechanism for completeness; enabling guest IPv6 is a separate product decision, not a hidden prerequisite for this fix. [Production classifier](../../crates/overdrive-bpf/src/programs/guest_tcx.rs).

One injected `HostSharedGuestNetworkOwner` currently owns shared convergence, startup probe, stale sweep, runtime audit, per-allocation provisioning, activation, quiescence, restore and teardown. Activation remains after intercept install success and before EXEC; the VMM adapter owns one per-launch TAP queue FD and the parent closes its duplicate. The production FD acquisition requests `IFF_TAP|IFF_NO_PI|IFF_VNET_HDR`, explicitly not multiqueue. None of routed TAP, TC forwarding or OVS kernel switching intrinsically requires changing that VM-facing Ethernet queue contract. [Owner](../../crates/overdrive-control-plane/src/guest_network.rs), [queue acquisition](../../crates/overdrive-netlink/src/client.rs), [ADR-0128](../product/architecture/adr-0128-vmm-adapter-owns-per-launch-tap-queue-descriptor.md).

### A. Routed TAP: precise proposed packet path and open decisions

A routed TAP still transports Ethernet; substituting TUN would change the VMM NIC contract unnecessarily. The host can own one shared guest gateway/DNS IP and a `/32` route for each guest through its standalone TAP. The guest may either retain its on-link prefix, using proxy ARP for peers, or be configured for host-route/default-gateway semantics. CNI's point-to-point plugin is primary precedent for deleting the automatically connected subnet route, adding a gateway host route and sending all other subnet traffic via the gateway, while the host installs a per-endpoint route. The plugin uses veth/netns, which are not copied here. [CNI v1.8.0 `ptp.go`](https://github.com/containernetworking/plugins/blob/0e648479e11c2c6d9109b14fc0c9ac64c677861b/plugins/main/ptp/ptp.go), [Firecracker routed-TAP guide](https://github.com/firecracker-microvm/firecracker/blob/ab8181ed6a1e67426b75f9692b0128a9ea84dfd1/docs/network-setup.md).

**Gateway and ARP.** Stock proxy ARP answers for an eligible routable peer on another interface, subject to forwarding/media/sysctl checks; ARP for a local gateway is separate local-address handling. The kernel uses the ingress TAP's `dev_addr` in its reply. Therefore, “turn on proxy ARP” does **not** by itself preserve the current canonical bridge-MAC classification and shared gateway. DESIGN must pin one complete scheme: (a) a deliberately owned local gateway address plus known per-TAP router MAC and matching classifier expectations, preserving host-TAP MAC constraints; or (b) an explicit responder presenting the existing canonical gateway MAC. Gateway placement, local-delivery eligibility on an addressless TAP, `PACKET_HOST`, reply source MAC, proxy scope and guest routes must be proved together. This is an actual interface/semantic gap for replacement DESIGN, not license to add endpoint fields or callbacks while implementing. [Linux v7.0 `arp.c`](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/ipv4/arp.c), [v7.0 sysctl source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/Documentation/networking/ip-sysctl.rst), [the failed bare-broute probe](../feature/netns-density-295/spike/findings.md).

**VM-to-VM and Service VIPs.** TCP from either peer-MAC or gateway-MAC cases is still intercepted into leg-F and the existing capability/resolver/TLS path. A `/32` output route and registered neighbor record deliver only leg-S's decrypted plaintext to the chosen TAP; a FIB route is not authority to bypass mTLS. An off-subnet Service VIP still uses gateway delivery and preserves the original VIP/port for the existing resolver. Direct non-TCP peer bypass remains forbidden. A broad forwarding or NAT rule from a quick-start guide would violate these accepted distinctions. [ADR-0115](../product/architecture/adr-0115-tcx-endpoint-classification-feeds-transparent-mtls.md), [production classifier](../../crates/overdrive-bpf/src/programs/guest_tcx.rs), [Linux FIB](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/ipv4/fib_trie.c).

**Egress/ingress and independent guards.** Ordinary host ingress and egress remain Linux IP hooks; existing TPROXY and independent R18 IP guard have a plausible preservation path. The current bridge-family classifier-loss guard does not execute on a standalone routed TAP, so its replacement hook and exact rule/member contract must be designed. Linux v7.0 runs TC ingress before netfilter ingress and before a device RX handler; this makes a managed-TAP `netdev` ingress proof-mark guard a concrete candidate. Its priorities, mark clearing, ARP handling, double loss and IPv4 early drops require native proof. It is not approved code/API. [v7.0 receive order](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/core/dev.c), [current guard contract](../product/architecture/adr-0115-tcx-endpoint-classification-feeds-transparent-mtls.md), [R18](../product/architecture/adr-0139-intercept-mark-fails-closed-without-ip-nft-program.md).

**Neighbors, isolation and lifecycle.** Returning frames need the registered guest MAC on the exact TAP. A route-scoped kernel neighbor can provide it; deciding permanent registered entries versus validated dynamic resolution is part of DESIGN. Permanent entries avoid dynamic GC admission but still cost memory and require explicit ownership/removal. An unprivileged guest's ARP may not populate a different guest's route/interface. Keep existing source anti-spoofing, TAP egress MAC gate, down-until-intercept activation, audits, synchronous effect completion and cleanup complement; teardown includes only that allocation's route/neighbor, not arbitrary host tables. No route-system database or new restart protocol is implied: first test reconstruction from the existing allocation plans and kernel inventory under the existing owner. [Neighbor implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/core/neighbour.c), [current owner](../../crates/overdrive-control-plane/src/guest_network.rs), [ADR-0142](../product/architecture/adr-0142-guest-tap-egress-drops-frames-to-foreign-destination-mac.md).

**IPv6/NDP.** Routed IPv6 would require owned `/128` routes and an explicit gateway NDP/router-advertisement/static-route scheme; setting `proxy_ndp` alone is not full provisioning. The currently supported guest contract is IPv4, so no IPv6 feature is added for #295. [Kernel IPv6 sysctls](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/Documentation/networking/ip-sysctl.rst), [IPv6 neighbor discovery](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/ipv6/ndisc.c), [current EtherType gate](../../crates/overdrive-bpf/src/programs/guest_tcx.rs). **Confidence:** medium for this coherent proposed path; native feasibility still pending.

### B. Registered TC/eBPF forwarding: what is different from importing Cilium

The useful precedent is an owner-written endpoint record selecting both destination ifindex and guest MAC. Cilium rewrites L2/L3 state from `ep->node_mac`/`ep->mac`, then performs explicit local delivery. It also has a source ARP responder and chooses redirect versus stack traversal according to endpoint/device mode. There is no dependency on a bridge's local FDB entries or learning the guest MAC. [Pinned local delivery](https://github.com/cilium/cilium/blob/e99150f8d8f403eca51ed82138d4ae20a265c8f3/bpf/lib/local_delivery.h), [ARP responder](https://github.com/cilium/cilium/blob/e99150f8d8f403eca51ed82138d4ae20a265c8f3/bpf/lib/arp.h), [endpoint map](https://github.com/cilium/cilium/blob/e99150f8d8f403eca51ed82138d4ae20a265c8f3/pkg/maps/lxcmap/lxcmap.go).

For Overdrive, a TC-based responder/registered-delivery adapter could preserve the canonical router MAC and avoid per-guest kernel neighbor dependence. **TCP is not sent directly TAP→TAP**: it must be diverted to host IP/leg-F, then the host's deliberate leg-S output may be delivered to the recorded guest. A host delivery anchor and host-output interception of guest-prefix traffic need exact contracts; simply adding `bpf_redirect` to the present ingress program is not complete. Direct redirect can bypass later host IP/netfilter stages; its egress TCX and observer behavior must be measured. [Linux redirect implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/core/filter.c), [receive/transmit hook order](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/core/dev.c), [transparent-transport contract](../product/architecture/adr-0115-tcx-endpoint-classification-feeds-transparent-mtls.md).

This avoids the Linux-bridge port ceiling but introduces map admission, map coherence and L2/L3 parser/responder ownership. Keep policy, Service resolution, TLS identities and recovery meanings in their existing owners. Do not copy Cilium's veth-only peer redirect, Kubernetes lifecycle, uint16 endpoint identity or Envoy proxy stack. Kernel TC helpers use interface identity rather than a ten-bit bridge port index; configured map limits remain genuine software bounds and must be declared. IPv6 requires a separately approved NDP parser/responder and `/128` handling. The existing IPv4 map/classifier and same-owner lifecycle provide reuse, not proof of the new forwarding path. [Cilium IDs](https://github.com/cilium/cilium/blob/e99150f8d8f403eca51ed82138d4ae20a265c8f3/pkg/endpoint/id/id.go), [kernel helpers](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/core/filter.c), [existing map](../../crates/overdrive-bpf/src/maps/guest_tcx.rs). **Confidence:** medium; performance and fail-closed equivalence unproved.

### C. Upstream OVS module: real strengths and hidden bounds

`openvswitch.ko` is an independent upstream datapath, not renamed or patched Linux bridge source. A netdev vport installs an RX handler; the datapath extracts a key, looks up a flow and executes its actions; an internal port re-enters Linux networking. That gives a real route to preserve TAP ownership and host TPROXY without Linux-bridge STP port numbering. Stock kernel packaging and matched userspace/module versions are allowed. [v7.0 netdev vport](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/openvswitch/vport-netdev.c), [internal vport](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/openvswitch/vport-internal_dev.c), [Kconfig](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/openvswitch/Kconfig), [official datapath guide](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/Documentation/topics/datapath.rst).

Its source-level limits are more restrictive than a casual “32-bit netlink port” reading: the UAPI transports u32 while the kernel stores u16 and rejects `port_no >= USHRT_MAX`. Port zero is the local port, so there are at most **65534 ordinary ports per kernel datapath**, with internal/uplink ports consuming the same space. OVS v4.0.0's generic OpenFlow model still uses an ordinary 16-bit range below `0xff00`: **65279 possible ordinary numbers**, and the default allocator deliberately cycles only below **32768**. Explicit `ofport_request` can reach the upper valid range; selecting OpenFlow 1.3 does not silently widen OVS's internal allocator. Each requested port/result must be observed, not assumed from OVSDB ADD success. [Kernel UAPI](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/include/uapi/linux/openvswitch.h), [kernel storage/allocator](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/openvswitch/datapath.c), [OVS type semantics](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/include/openvswitch/types.h), [OpenFlow range](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/include/openflow/openflow-1.0.h), [allocator](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/ofproto/ofproto.c). **Confidence: high at the pinned versions.**

Standard `ovs-vswitchd` merges same-type logical switches into a common `ovs-system` datapath. Adding more OVS logical bridges does **not** multiply the kernel's available port IDs. The kernel can host multiple datapaths, but bypassing the standard daemon and controlling them directly would create a different owner/control implementation and is not assumed. [OVS shared backer](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/ofproto/ofproto-dpif.c), [official design FAQ](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/Documentation/faq/design.rst).

A feasible #295 OVS configuration must explicitly choose registered output ports/MACs, gateway/ARP behavior, TCP-to-internal-host delivery and default drops; `NORMAL` learning/flooding by itself is not sufficient. TCX may still validate source packets before the OVS RX handler and retain the destination gate at TAP egress, but **the nft bridge-family guard is not an OVS guard**. A separate hook must still cover classifier loss. Flow misses may upcall to userspace; cache revalidation, flow insertion/removal and daemon/module loss become things the current owner must truthfully observe and gate. ARP/NDP broadcast can be supported but must not become an unauthorized direct guest transport. Host/internal-port IP delivery must preserve marks/original destination and shared DNS. Existing all-interface/capture-before-first-VMM evidence must include the OVS internal port, not assume visibility from a Linux bridge observer. [Linux hook order](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/core/dev.c), [official datapath guide](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/Documentation/topics/datapath.rst), [existing capture contract](../product/architecture/adr-0115-tcx-endpoint-classification-feeds-transparent-mtls.md).

OVSDB and `ovs-vswitchd` are genuine packaging/ownership dependencies, not automatic disqualifiers. **Startup authority is concrete at the pinned OVS version:** `open_dpif_backer` enumerates same-type datapaths and deletes each successfully opened datapath whose name differs from its own backer name (`ofproto-dpif.c:760–779`). Candidate C therefore requires isolated daemon-startup and cleanup observations that demonstrate the retained foreign-state complement before selection; the operation must fit the exact approved owner/lifecycle contract. This is source evidence about the candidate, with no established reachability through current Overdrive production. [Pinned startup path](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/ofproto/ofproto-dpif.c). DESIGN must decide how the existing injected owner treats configuration intent, kernel realization and foreign configuration without creating a second authoritative allocation registry. No new persistent protocol is required by the research result. This is broader integration than A; its justification would be measured native advantages or L2 product requirements, not elegance. **Confidence:** medium for integration feasibility; no Overdrive OVS native measurement was run.

### D. Multiple Linux bridges: bounded composition, not a magic capacity fix

With one uplink per leaf, at most **1022 guest TAPs fit each leaf**. At the unchanged illustrative N=16384, `ceil(16384/1022)=17` leaves plus one root and 17 veth uplink pairs would be a possible L2 tree. This is arithmetic from the allocator, **not an approved topology or capacity receipt**. A root's own 1023 port slots impose another finite bound; any external ports reduce its budget. Routed independent leaves avoid a root L2 port allocator but need explicit prefix/gateway/routes and cease to be one broadcast segment. [Linux bridge allocator](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/bridge/br_if.c), [bridge forwarding/flooding](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/bridge/br_forward.c).

The L2 tree retains peer ARP and gateway delivery, but needs exact local-MAC/gateway placement, shared-owner membership, TCX/guard hooks on each managed source TAP and egress delivery on every target TAP. The same bridge host-MAC/FDB poisoning proof remains relevant; the root and leaves duplicate forwarding state and a shared broadcast domain still creates aggregate O(N) replication. A direct bridged TCP bypass stays forbidden even across leaves; Service VIPs and host legs still use existing TPROXY/TLS semantics. Cleanup/restart must enumerate only owned leaves/uplinks and preserve foreign links; adding leaves cannot create a new weaker activation or recovery window. [MAC/FDB native proof](../feature/netns-density-295/spike/findings-mac-fdb-isolation.md), [accepted security contract](../product/architecture/adr-0115-tcx-endpoint-classification-feeds-transparent-mtls.md), [FDB source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/bridge/br_fdb.c).

One bridge per workload is reopened where its density-based rejection depended on the falsified shared-switch premise. It still creates one extra netdevice per guest plus route/gateway objects; neither a single-leaf failure domain nor object count proves it superior. A bounded shared-leaf topology is a distinct comparison option, not a mandate to replace existing ownership with shards or a persistent placement service. **Confidence:** high for port arithmetic, medium for comparative work/cost, low for target performance.

### E/F and other screened mechanisms

Netmap/VALE is a real independent module. Its README distinguishes core/VALE and generic access to unmodified drivers from native driver patches; the Linux-specific README's older emphasis on patched drivers must not be treated as permission to patch this appliance kernel. Only generic/unmodified-driver operation is admissible here. However, the pinned VALE constant is `NM_BDG_MAXPORTS=254`, and its ring/netmap API is not the existing CH `--net fd=` TAP interface. Batching throughput reported by the original research does not change either structural limit or prove the required host TLS/observer path. [VALE constants](https://github.com/luigirizzo/netmap/blob/389daeaf498e4928e2070e5002590108c84e2f39/sys/dev/netmap/netmap_bdg.h), [core/generic statement](https://github.com/luigirizzo/netmap/blob/389daeaf498e4928e2070e5002590108c84e2f39/README.md), [generic implementation](https://github.com/luigirizzo/netmap/blob/389daeaf498e4928e2070e5002590108c84e2f39/sys/dev/netmap/netmap_generic.c), [original USENIX paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc12/technical-sessions/presentation/rizzo).

OVS userspace `netdev` mode is real and uses stock kernel packet/TUN support. DPDK/vhost-user additionally changes VM queue custody and may take the physical NIC from the kernel; getting traffic back into host IP/TPROXY is a deliberate exception path, not automatic compatibility. Shared guest memory/backend sockets, hugepages/buffers and polling cores become appliance obligations; standard OpenFlow port bounds still exist. Those costs are not a packaging prohibition, but no current native result proves they are required for the #295 outcome. [OVS userspace installation](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/Documentation/intro/install/userspace.rst), [OVS vhost-user ports](https://github.com/openvswitch/ovs/blob/b89dc80f0143f5bf3f58c48d54e82cc7f8d29da8/Documentation/topics/dpdk/vhost-user.rst), [CH existing TAP handoff](https://github.com/cloud-hypervisor/cloud-hypervisor/blob/ae14eb5d8a4612efd8e1ed389da94a5c401c1d93/vmm/src/device_manager.rs).

Stock `macvtap`/`macvlan` and SR-IOV are not blanket rejected as modules. The relevant failure is path compatibility: macvlan transmit selects peer/lowerdev output, rather than the required host IP prerouting interception path; a physical VF assigned directly to a guest similarly bypasses that owner boundary. Neither is a drop-in replacement for the currently proven transparent mTLS/capture mechanism. An explicit host-redirection design could be evaluated if needed, but that would be another candidate with its own proof, not inherited feasibility. [v7.0 macvlan transmit](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/drivers/net/macvlan.c), [earlier bounded NIC-interface research](dataplane/guest-nic-attachment-netns-vs-fd-passing-comprehensive-research.md), [kernel PCI IOV guide](https://docs.kernel.org/PCI/pci-iov-howto.html).

A newly authored independent module using stock registered netdevice/RX-handler interfaces is permitted in principle; there is no demonstrated requirement for it after A/B/C. Its identifier widths, packet ownership, guard hooks, memory bounds and compatibility would be **unverified**, so this report does not invent a “limitless module” as a recommendation. Rebuilding a patched Linux bridge under another module name remains expressly forbidden. [Stock netdevice/RX-handler implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.0/net/core/dev.c).

## Cloudflare prior art: three different boundaries

The user requested Cloudflare specifically during research. The Cloudflare skill was read for its retrieval guidance; current official docs, first-party engineering descriptions and published code were then checked. ### Workers and V8 isolates

Workers execute in V8 isolates within an existing runtime rather than one VM or TAP per invocation. Their density removes per-VM kernel/virtio/network-object costs by choosing a different execution boundary. It does not prove Overdrive can support an equivalent count of Cloud Hypervisor guests, nor supply guest ARP/NDP, queue-FD isolation, gateway identity or host cleanup. **Applicability:** useful evidence that execution-unit choice changes the resource model; no topology transfer. [Official Workers execution model](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/reference/how-workers-works/). **Confidence:** high for the product distinction; no relevance to a numeric Overdrive density promise.

### Containers and Sandbox microVM networking

The current Containers lifecycle documentation says each instance is a Firecracker microVM with a separate kernel/network; requests pass through Workers/Durable Objects, and end-users do not obtain arbitrary inbound TCP/UDP. These are explicit isolation and gateway boundaries, not disclosure of a host TAP switch. The 2024 platform article describes runtime choice (gVisor, Firecracker, QEMU and evaluating Cloud Hypervisor/vhost-user) and scheduling by available hardware; it does not publish the guest attachment allocator. [Current lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/concepts/architecture/), [2024 platform engineering account](https://blog.cloudflare.com/container-platform-preview/).

Current outbound handlers cover HTTP/HTTPS on ports 80/443; other ports require internet access or are denied. DNS for disabled-internet mode returns only policy-covered placeholder destinations. Its documented local-development implementation uses a per-container-netns sidecar and TPROXY toward local workerd. **Applicability:** confirms deliberate gateway/proxy policy and trusted credentials outside untrusted execution, but this local-development topology does not establish production host netns/bridge choice, and HTTP-specific interception is narrower than Overdrive's protected TCP contract. [Official outbound behavior and local-development path](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/configuration/outbound-traffic/), [Sandbox security](https://developers.cloudflare.com/sandbox/concepts/security/).

The published limits are **product/account quotas**, not host fanout: current predefined sizes range from fractional vCPU/256 MiB to 4 vCPU/12 GiB; account concurrency includes 1500 vCPU and 6 TiB memory. No inspected public source identifies host bridge/port bounds, TAP route/neighbor scale, pre-VMM capture receipts, kernel-version attestation or exact host cleanup-complement/restart protocol. Therefore a Cloudflare VM host design cannot be claimed from these docs. [Limits, updated 2026-09-30](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform/limits/). **Confidence:** high for stated product behavior; unknown for private host datapath and per-machine bounds.

The 2020 Unimog engineering account describes physical-server load balancing: ECMP ingress, XDP forwarding with `XDP_TX`, GUE encapsulation and a TC redirector that can keep established connections on the previous server. It describes replacing GitHub's `glb-redirect` module with TC, partly for deployment and pre-processing tcpdump visibility. This is not VM switching or proof of a host TAP population. Its router ECMP, CPU/load, forwarding maps, NIC and tunnel/MTU constraints remain real. No published single-machine endpoint/map capacity is stated for its production XDP program. [Cloudflare Unimog, David Wragg, 2020-09-09](https://blog.cloudflare.com/unimog-cloudflares-edge-load-balancer/). **Confidence:** high for the historical first-party mechanism, low for current undisclosed deployment limits.

[Project](https://github.com/cloudflare/tubular/blob/35817992491457fd026d3c69bf4daa498360d94c/README.md), [dispatcher](https://github.com/cloudflare/tubular/blob/35817992491457fd026d3c69bf4daa498360d94c/ebpf/inet-kern.c), [owner loader](https://github.com/cloudflare/tubular/blob/35817992491457fd026d3c69bf4daa498360d94c/internal/dispatcher.go). **Confidence:** high for source-level bounds; no VM-density applicability.

### New candidate G: shared-memory/vsock communication with a deliberate host proxy

This is a serious extreme-density candidate because its class of transport can remove **per-VM host netdevices, bridge port IDs, FDB/neighbor state and their host lock/scanner work**, rather than raising one switch constant. It does not intrinsically require a Linux patch: current Cloud Hypervisor already implements stream virtio-vsock via a per-VM Unix-socket backend, and Overdrive's current launcher already supplies `--vsock cid=3` with its allocation-specific path. Standard shared virtqueues/guest-host channels are real capabilities; the private Unikraft implementation is not assumed to be identical.
